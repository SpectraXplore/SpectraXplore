Why Multiscale GCN

Beyond CNNs and Standard Graph Networks

Conventional CNNs struggle with the irregular geometry and complex topological relationships found in real mineral systems, and standard Graph Convolutional Networks have limited capacity to capture features across scales — leaving datasets siloed, receptive fields fixed, and information lost between layers. SpectraXplore's multiscale GCN was purpose-built for mineral prospectivity mapping to close that gap.

  • Hierarchical feature learning — from regional faults down to deposit-scale geochemistry
  • Geological topology awareness — models the complex relationships within a mineral system rather than a flat pixel grid
  • Integration of heterogeneous data — geophysical, geochemical, remote-sensing and geological evidence on one graph
  • Robust in data-sparse environments — where fixed-grid CNNs and single-scale GCNs typically fall short
// COMPUTER VISION MODEL — BI-CLSTM FOR HYPERSPECTRAL IMAGERY

Alongside multiscale GCN, SpectraXplore™ can build a Bidirectional-Convolutional LSTM (Bi-CLSTM) network for spectral-spatial feature learning on hyperspectral imagery. Spectral feature extraction across a scene's many narrow bands is treated as a sequence-learning problem, using a recurrent connection across the spectral domain, run in both directions to capture the full spectral context — while a convolutional operator across the spatial domain extracts spatial structure, in the same spirit as a CNN. The two feature sets are concatenated and passed to a classifier, giving stronger classification performance on hyperspectral scenes than CNN-only approaches.
